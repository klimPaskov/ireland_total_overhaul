# Ireland Country Package — Research and Classification

## Source method

Mechanics use three labels:

- **Documented history (H):** supported by contemporary law, parliamentary
  debate, official statistics, archives, institutional records, or strong
  historical scholarship.
- **Plausible divergence (P):** a counterfactual built from a real actor,
  institution, dispute, capability, or documented proposal.
- **Deliberate absurdity (A):** impossible or supernatural content. It is hidden,
  explicitly permission-gated, and reached through serious cultural research.

`P` content must still obey material capacity, domestic opposition, foreign
reaction, and wartime logistics. `A` content cannot be presented as historical
fact or appear in historical AI games.

## Documented national baseline

### Population and economy

- The 1936 census recorded a population just under three million. Agricultural
  employment, rural settlement, small holdings, emigration, housing quality, and
  uneven regional development define the opening economy.
- Contemporary Dáil debate treated the Economic War as a crisis linking cattle,
  pigs, exports, employment, consumer prices, public revenue, and the viability
  of industrial policy. This supports a multi-part agricultural/export system,
  not a single stability modifier.
- Ireland had real state-building institutions to expand: ESB, the Turf
  Development Board, Irish Sugar, Aer Lingus, the railway network, and protected
  domestic manufacturing. Their limits matter: capital, imported machinery,
  coal, shipping, distribution, and demand.
- Aer Lingus began operations in 1936. Irish Shipping was incorporated in March
  1941 to secure essential imports. Rural electrification belongs to the 1946
  postwar programme, not the 1936 opening.

### Constitutional politics

- The 1937 Constitution was enacted by the people on 1 July and came into force
  on 29 December 1937. It reorganized state offices and external authority while
  preserving parliamentary government.
- The 1938 Anglo-Irish settlement ended the Economic War, transferred the Treaty
  Ports, and involved a financial settlement. It therefore affects sovereignty,
  budget, agriculture, defence, and British relations simultaneously.
- Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael, Labour, independents, agrarian interests, the Catholic
  hierarchy, the civil service, trade unions, and republican networks are
  distinct actors. Their influence cannot be reduced to one ideology percentage.
- The first inter-party government in 1948 joined Fine Gael, Labour, Clann na
  Poblachta, Clann na Talmhan, and independents. The Republic of Ireland Act 1948
  repealed the External Relations Act and assigned external executive functions
  to the President. These are late-game constitutional and coalition outcomes.

### Emergency and security

- The Emergency Powers Act 1939 authorized broad control of essential supplies,
  trade, navigation, communications, property, movement, and publication. It did
  not authorize conscription or participation in war without Dáil assent.
- Neutrality required active enforcement: internment, censorship, airspace and
  maritime incidents, intelligence, rescue, trade, and relations with both
  belligerent camps.
- The Coast Watching Service operated 83 lookout posts from 1939 to 1945. The
  Defence Forces, Local Defence Force, G2, Garda, ports, airfields, merchant
  shipping, and local observation networks supply distinct gameplay projects.
- The IRA's wartime campaign, state repression, German contacts, internment, and
  internal republican divisions support serious security and revolutionary
  routes. Political violence and repression are not comic flavour.

### Partition and foreign policy

- Partition is the central territorial question, but Northern Ireland contains
  unionist, nationalist, labour, church, local-government, industrial, and
  British-state interests. A border outcome must address those interests and the
  integration burden.
- Irish neutrality coexisted with practical pressures and limited cooperation.
  Alignment, access, intelligence, and supply are separate decisions.
- External expansion beyond Northern Ireland is counterfactual. Claims in the
  Highlands, Wales, or Cornwall belong only to explicitly alternate ideological
  campaigns and require diplomatic demands, naval reach, readiness, occupation,
  and postwar settlement. The Isle of Man is not a separate vanilla state and is
  not promised as a state claim.

### Postwar statecraft

- Rural electrification began in 1946 and ran through the following decade.
- Demobilisation, emergency-power rollback, housing, emigration, welfare,
  coalition government, republic status, trade access, and reconstruction
  provide a genuine 1946–1951 campaign rather than a victory screen in 1941.
- Ireland's unsuccessful immediate postwar UN application and continuing
  neutrality debates support diplomatic conclusions short of automatic faction
  alignment.

## Plausible political families

| Family | Historical roots | Plausible divergence boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| democratic republican | Fianna Fáil, Fine Gael, Labour, agrarian and coalition politics | different coalition, successor, social programme, external posture, or republican settlement |
| Congress socialist | Republican Congress, labour republicanism, trade unions and the 1919 Democratic Programme | Congress survives fragmentation, builds civilian institutions, and contests government |
| Blueshirt authoritarian | Army Comrades Association/Blueshirts, O'Duffy, corporatist and anti-communist currents | an emergency coalition converts veteran networks into a state project |
| producer-estate oligarchy | vocational constitutional arguments, professional bodies, agricultural producers, clerical social thought | franchise and executive power move toward organized estates without inventing a mass fascist party |
| constitutional restoration | Gaelic revival, historical scholarship, symbolic presidency, cultural societies | a public constitutional movement creates a ceremonial or constrained restored office |

No family receives a generic ideology switch. It must build institutions,
survive opposition, and settle state authority.

## Radical and hidden classification

- IRA military government and Ailtirí total-state content are `P` only where
  built from real organizations and wartime conditions; invented successes and
  institutions must be labelled speculative.
- High Kingship is `P` while ceremonial, constitutional, or political. It becomes
  `A` only when impossible sovereignty or sacral power is accepted.
- Five Provinces and Otherworld content begins with `H` cultural evidence,
  becomes `P` during political interpretation and containment, and becomes `A`
  only after verified impossible events.
- Absurd routes require rare permission, multiple completed cultural projects,
  specific world or territorial conditions, and a disqualifier/cleanup model.

## Claims that require caution

- Do not infer mass public support for marginal ideological organizations from
  their existence.
- Do not use later organizations, company names, flags, or offices before their
  documented date unless the route explicitly founds an alternate predecessor.
- Do not treat folklore, medieval titles, or archaeological sites as proof of a
  1930s political programme.
- Do not turn Northern communities into a single value. Settlement legitimacy,
  local administration, security, labour, industry, and British response remain
  separate concerns.
