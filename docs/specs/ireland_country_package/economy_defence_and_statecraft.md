# Ireland Country Package — Economy, Defence, and Statecraft

## Reward standard

Every focus reward belongs to at least one class:

1. **Material:** equipment, fuel, convoys, trains, construction, state building,
   unit placement, or access to a production organization.
2. **Capability:** technology/doctrine bonus, MIO growth, advisor/company/law,
   agency/intelligence, unit role, production method, or persistent modifier.
3. **Playable operation:** named decision/mission/event chain with real inputs,
   success/failure, and cleanup.
4. **Institutional transformation:** a visible multi-stage idea, office, balance,
   or country identity whose current effects are shown to the player.
5. **Route commitment:** a clearly labelled mutually exclusive choice that
   immediately changes available gameplay.

A helper flag is implementation state, not a player reward. A ledger delta is
acceptable only when the ledger is visible and the tooltip prints the direction
and magnitude or resulting stage.

## National development programmes

### Agriculture and the Economic War

Projects:

- cattle/export settlement, domestic tillage, seed and fertilizer procurement;
- smallholder credit, rural price stabilization, cooperative storage;
- emergency acreage and rationing during the war;
- postwar mechanization and migration/employment response.

Inputs include civilian factory use, trains, fuel, agricultural support, British
market access, and seasonal missions. Outcomes include supply, consumer-goods
pressure, construction speed, manpower movement, and rural political support.

### Power and peat

- ESB grid surveys and industrial connection contracts;
- Turf Development Board drainage, mechanical extraction, rail spurs, and fuel
  reserve;
- 1946 rural electrification programme, region-by-region rather than an instant
  national modifier.

Failures include capital shortage, poor transport economics, equipment import
delay, and labour/housing strain. Route governments decide ownership and control.

### Transport and ports

- Great Southern Railways maintenance and rolling-stock acquisition;
- Dublin/Cork/port works, Treaty Port transfer and defence conversion;
- supply hubs, rail links, trains, and convoy handling tied to actual states;
- postwar road/housing portfolio only after wartime bottlenecks are resolved.

### Manufacturing and companies

The nine capability focuses unlock seven concerns and two MIOs:

- ESB, Irish Sugar, Turf Development Board, Great Southern Railways, Aer Lingus,
  Irish Shipping, Irish Steel;
- Ford Cork motorized/mechanized MIO;
- Harland and Wolff Belfast naval MIO after lawful Northern access.

Each has one distinct contract operation with material requirements and output.
No focus merely shows an idea without a contract or production consequence.

## Defence architecture

### Army lane

Sequence: command audit -> officer corps/civilian oversight -> equipment standard
-> mobilization model -> field exercises -> route doctrine -> late-war force.

Concrete outputs include doctrine cost reduction, infantry/support equipment,
command power, army XP, a bounded trained unit where historically and materially
justified, and decision access for exercises or mobilization. Route doctrines
change force composition rather than only attack/defence percentages.

### Air lane

Sequence: warning network -> Air Corps procurement -> radar/observation -> airfield
programme -> interception doctrine. Aer Lingus supplies transport experience and
air links; it does not substitute for military aviation.

### Maritime lane

Sequence: Marine Service reserve -> coastal patrol -> naval staff -> coastwatch and
ports -> convoy office -> Irish Shipping -> route maritime posture. It belongs on
the outer defence side of the tree, not between politics and industry.

Concrete outputs include convoys, patrol craft or bounded naval equipment,
dockyard/port/radar construction, Navy XP, naval research, and convoy-protection
operations.

### Intelligence and internal security

G2, Garda, censorship, IRA policy, foreign agents, rescue/interment, and covert
cooperation form an active security system. Repression has consent, legitimacy,
foreign, and radicalization costs. Leniency has infiltration and readiness risks.

## Neutrality and diplomacy

Neutrality is maintained through incidents, not completed by a single focus.

| Posture | Benefits | Costs/risks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| strict legal neutrality | legitimacy, balanced diplomacy | weaker equipment and intelligence access |
| armed neutrality | readiness and deterrence | high supply and mobilization burden |
| benevolent neutrality | Allied intelligence/equipment | German pressure and domestic republican backlash |
| formal democratic security | guarantees and interoperability | neutrality loss and wartime exposure |
| revolutionary internationalism | labour/republican support | trade and diplomatic isolation |
| authoritarian sponsorship | equipment and doctrine channels | dependence, infiltration, legitimacy loss |

Incident families: airspace, U-boats/shipping, rescue, internment, weather reports,
intelligence, volunteers, trade cargoes, port access, diplomatic representation,
and censorship. Each option states the visible effect and future consequence.

## Postwar programme

The emergency ends through decisions and focuses:

- demobilize forces and resolve emergency laws;
- settle censorship/internment/security powers;
- rural electrification and housing portfolio;
- coalition or succession government;
- Republic of Ireland/external-relations settlement;
- UN application or alternate diplomatic order;
- migration, employment, welfare, and industrial export choices.

The conclusion opens continuing investment, settlement, and foreign-policy
decisions so the campaign does not end when the capstone completes.

## Family postwar settlements

After 1946, completed emergency rollback, demobilisation, reconstruction,
constitutional status, and international-order work unlocks one 180-day family
settlement project. Democratic government ratifies the postwar republic;
Congress civilianises wartime command; Blueshirts place the directorate under
statute or demobilise it; producer estates convert contracts into reconstruction
credit; restoration defines the postwar crown. Each reserves one civilian
factory and produces an institutional settlement rather than free equipment,
factories, cores, or generic stability.
